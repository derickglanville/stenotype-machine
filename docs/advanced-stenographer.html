Plover steno decoder ring

Keep this reference beside your iPad while using Verbatim. The aim is to see a steno outline as sounds and sections, rather than a random collection of letters.

Read each stroke by its structure

A stroke follows this basic structure. Press every key in one stroke simultaneously. A slash means release, then make another stroke.

LEFT HAND → VOWELS → RIGHT HAND S T K P W H R A O * E U F R P B L G T S D Z └── beginning ──┘ └──── ending ────┘

4. Read a chord as sections

For COURT → KO*URT, divide the outline as K | OU | * | RT: beginning, vowel, modifier, ending. For DAMAGES → TKAPBLGS, begin with TK for D and recognize the remainder as the theory’s compressed representation of the word. Learn sound patterns, not a strange spelling system.

5. A slash means another stroke

AFFIDAVIT → AF/TKA*EUFT means stroke AF, release, then stroke TKA*EUFT. ARBITRATION → AR/PWEU/TRAEUGS means three separate strokes. Count strokes, rather than the number of displayed characters, when judging efficiency.

6. The star is special

The * key is not pronounced. Treat it as a modifier. Plover dictionaries use it to distinguish outlines, create briefs, handle alternate translations, and other dictionary-specific functions. Learn it as part of the chord when the dictionary requires it.

Practice progression

  1. Word + chord + explanation + highlighted keys
  2. Word + chord + highlighted keys
  3. Word + chord
  4. Word only
  5. Spoken word only
  6. Continuous sentences

The goal is to progress from hearing “breach,” visualizing the word, then remembering an outline, toward directly hearing “breach” and producing the chord.
